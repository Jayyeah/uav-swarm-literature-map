# READING NOTE — P0042 — Dual-policy target allocation and spatially valid AUV capture

## 1. Paper positioning
- Problem: [原文] Multiple AUVs must allocate themselves into target-oriented subgroups and continuously maneuver in 3-D until multiple maneuvering underwater targets satisfy a physically stricter encirclement/capture criterion.
- Main predecessors: [原文] Multi-AUV hunting/allocation; graph-based multi-robot assignment; MARL pursuit/hunting; differentiable Sinkhorn assignment; encirclement criteria based on distance/angles/enclosure quality.
- Why this paper was needed: [原文] Existing learning approaches often leave target assignment implicit/distance-based/reward-guided, while classical modular solutions separate assignment, pursuit and capture assessment. The paper explicitly couples discrete allocation with continuous subgroup control.
- [AI判断] This is the **strongest structural CoCap competitor in R-LM-01** because it already contains learned target preferences, hard capacity-feasible assignment, assignment-conditioned subgroup control, and a nontrivial multi-agent capture definition.

## 2. Problem setting
- Agents / targets: [原文] Multiple AUVs vs multiple maneuvering targets in a bounded 3-D cube \(\mathcal W=[0,L]^3\).
- Dynamics: [原文] Simplified 3-D AUV kinematics with yaw, pitch, forward thrust, velocity attenuation, command smoothing, energy state and uniform-current drift.
- Action space: [原文] Continuous \(u_i^t=[u_{i,T}^t,u_{i,\psi}^t,u_{i,\theta}^t]^T\in[-1,1]^3\): normalized thrust, yaw-rate and pitch-rate commands.
- Sensing / observation: [原文] Actor input is called a “local observation” and includes self, target and teammate features. Crucially, Assumption 1 states **target positions are available to the pursuer team through external support**. Every target appears in the target feature set, together with estimated velocity, EKF predictions, observability flag, capture status and previous assignment.
- Communication: [原文] Teammate features contain a connectivity indicator; however the paper deliberately simplifies communication and assumes externally supported target positions. It does not implement CoCap-style strict local target sensing/neighbor-only target dissemination.
- Centralized/decentralized: [原文] CTDE. A centralized critic uses global state only during training. Actor factorization is allocation policy + per-AUV assignment-conditioned control policies at execution.
- Map / obstacles: [原文] Bounded 3-D water volume with uniform current; no obstacle field in the reported setup.
- Target behavior: [原文] Reactive evasion with inertial persistence, filtered stochastic wandering, repulsion from nearby active AUVs and wall avoidance; not learned/self-played.
- Speed ratio: [原文] Table 1: max AUV speed 1.5 vs max target speed 0.7, so pursuers have a substantial speed advantage, consistent with Assumption 2.
- Capture / termination definition: [原文] “Spatially valid capture” requires sufficient participating AUVs, 3-D convex-hull enclosure, angular/formation balance and positive dynamic closing advantage simultaneously. After capture, the assigned subgroup is frozen/held and **is not reassigned**; episode continues until all fixed targets are captured or the step limit is reached.

## 3. Core idea
### Plain-language summary
[原文] The actor is decomposed into two coupled decisions. First, an allocation network scores every AUV–target pair and a Sinkhorn capacity layer turns those scores into a hard feasible assignment. Second, the same relational encoder is rerun using the assignment so the control network emphasizes the selected target and same-subgroup teammates. Both policies are jointly optimized with PPO under CTDE.

[AI判断] This is much closer than TERL to true **assignment + subgroup control**. However, the capacity is prescribed in advance; it does not learn whether a particular coalition is actually sufficient to capture a target from the current state.

### Mathematical / algorithmic mechanism
#### Motion model, Eqs. (1)–(8)
[原文] Raw commands are first-order smoothed:

\[
\tilde u_i^t=\alpha \hat u_i^t+(1-\alpha)\tilde u_i^{t-1}. \tag{1}
\]

Yaw/pitch integrate the commanded rates (Eq. 2), the heading vector is Eq. (3), pre-saturation velocity is

\[
\bar v_i^{t+1}=\eta v_i^t+\hat d_i^{t+1}\tilde u_{i,T}^t a_{max}\Delta t, \tag{4}
\]

and position includes current drift:

\[
p_i^{t+1}=\operatorname{clip}_{\mathcal W}(p_i^t+v_i^{t+1}\Delta t+c). \tag{5}
\]

[原文] Target Eqs. (7)–(8) mix inertia, stochastic wandering, repulsion and wall avoidance.

#### Spatially valid capture, Eqs. (9)–(17)
Let \(\mathcal H_j\) be the subgroup assigned to target \(j\), and \(\{h_i\}_{i=1}^{N_h}\) the participating hunters within capture distance \(r_c\).

1. Participation: \(N_h\ge N_{min}\).
2. Geometric enclosure, Eq. (9):

\[
p_j^T\in \operatorname{conv}(\{h_i\}_{i=1}^{N_h}).
\]

3. Formation balance. Unit vectors from hunters to target, Eq. (10):

\[
e_i=\frac{p_j^T-h_i}{\|p_j^T-h_i\|}.
\]

Formation quality, Eq. (11):

\[
\kappa=\frac{\left\|\sum_{i=1}^{N_h}e_i\right\|}{N_h},\qquad \kappa<\kappa_{th}.
\]

4. Dynamic closing advantage. Subgroup center \(c_j\), outward direction \(e_{out}\), target outward escape component and mean hunter closing speed are Eqs. (12)–(15):

\[
v_{escape}=\max(0,(v_j^T)^Te_{out}),
\]

\[
v_{close}=\frac{1}{N_h}\sum_i v_i^T e_i,
\]

and Eq. (16)

\[
m=v_{close}-v_{escape}>0.
\]

Combined Eq. (17):

\[
Capture=(N_h\ge N_{min})\land(p_j^T\in conv(\{h_i\}))\land(\kappa<\kappa_{th})\land(m>0).
\]

[原文] Adopted parameters include \(r_c=10\), \(N_{min}=4\), \(\kappa_{th}=0.5\), closing threshold 0.

[AI判断] This is one of the strongest **capture semantics** in the learning batch. It is still not equivalent to the R-PE-01 notion of a capturability certificate: Eq. (17) recognizes a good terminal/current configuration; it does not quantify a strategy that guarantees eventual entry into that set against every admissible evader response.

## 4. Method details
### RL/MARL papers
- Observation: [原文] Eq. (18):

\[
o_i^t=[s_{i}^{self,t},s_i^{tar,t},s_i^{mate,t}].
\]

  Self feature Eq. (19): normalized position/velocity, yaw, pitch, residual energy, frozen flag.
  Target feature Eq. (20), for **every target**: target-relative position, estimated target velocity, three EKF future-position predictions, observability indicator \(\delta_{ij}^t\), capture-status indicator \(c_j^t\), previous assignment \(z_{ij}^{t-1}\).
  Teammate Eq. (21): relative position, velocity, frozen flag and connectivity indicator for every teammate.
- Action: [原文] Mixed discrete/continuous: assignment \(z_i^t\) + continuous thrust/yaw/pitch command \(u_i^t\).
- Reward: [原文] Eqs. (30)–(36), detailed below.
- Architecture: [原文] Shared allocation-aware GAT relational encoder + allocation actor + continuous control actor + centralized critic.
- Actor/Critic/Q: [原文] PPO-style stochastic actor with centralized state-value critic.
- CTDE: [原文] Yes.
- Replay/on-policy: [原文] On-policy rollouts, PPO + GAE.
- Curriculum/exploration: [原文] No staged task curriculum analogous to TERL; assignment/control are jointly trained. Allocation/control entropy regularization is used.

### Allocation mechanism
High-level assignment for AUV \(i\), Eq. (22):

\[
z_i^t=[z_{i1}^t,\ldots,z_{iN_T}^t],\quad z_{ij}^t\in\{0,1\},\quad \sum_j z_{ij}^t=1.
\]

Team matrix, Eq. (23):

\[
Z^t=[z_{ij}^t]\in\{0,1\}^{N_A\times N_T}.
\]

[原文] Allocation policy outputs logits \(b_i^t=\pi_\theta^{alloc}(g_i^t)\in\mathbb R^{N_T}\), Eq. (25).

Let capacity vector \(q=[q_1,\ldots,q_{N_T}]^T\), with prescribed target subgroup demand \(q_j\) and

\[
\sum_j q_j=N_A.
\]

Sinkhorn soft assignment approximately satisfies Eq. (26):

\[
\sum_jP_{soft,ij}^t\approx1,\qquad \sum_iP_{soft,ij}^t\approx q_j.
\]

[原文] Greedy capacity rounding creates an executable binary matrix \(P_{hard}\). The straight-through estimator, Eq. (27), is

\[
Z^t=P_{hard}^t-\operatorname{sg}(P_{soft}^t)+P_{soft}^t,
\]

so the forward pass is hard/capacity-feasible and the backward pass differentiates through the soft matrix.

[原文] Base experiment uses 8 AUVs / 2 targets with capacity 4 per target: total AUV supply exactly equals total prescribed demand.

### Allocation-aware relational encoder
- [原文] **Phase 1:** before current allocation, GAT encodes ego/target/teammate relations into allocation feature \(g_i^t\).
- [原文] **Phase 2:** after \(Z^t\) is produced, the same GAT weights are reused with allocation-conditioned relation types and graph masks, emphasizing the assigned target and same-subgroup teammates to form control feature \(f_i^t\).
- [AI判断] This is a strong architectural pattern for linking a discrete team decision to continuous control without separate handcrafted subgroup controllers.

### Control actor and joint policy
[原文] Eq. (28) outputs Gaussian mean \(\mu_i^t=\pi_\theta^{ctrl}(f_i^t)\); Eq. (29):

\[
\pi_\theta^u(u_i^t|o_i^t,z_i^t)=\mathcal N(\mu_i^t,\operatorname{diag}(\sigma^2)).
\]

Joint factorization Eq. (37):

\[
\pi_\theta(Z^t,U^t|\mathcal O^t)=\pi_\theta^z(Z^t|\mathcal O^t)\prod_{i=1}^{N_A}\pi_\theta^u(u_i^t|o_i^t,z_i^t).
\]

[原文] Centralized critic uses global state during training; PPO clipped objective is Eq. (38), combined actor/critic/entropy/allocation objective Eq. (39).

### Reward, Eqs. (30)–(36)
For AUV \(i\) assigned to target \(j_i^t\):

- Shell/convergence reward Eq. (30):

\[
r_{i,shell}^t=\lambda_{conv}(|d_i^{t-1}-R_{enc}|-|d_i^t-R_{enc}|)+\lambda_{prox}\exp\left(-\frac{|d_i^t-R_{enc}|}{\sigma_R}\right).
\]

- Radial squeeze Eq. (31):

\[
r_{i,squeeze}^t=\mathbf 1[d_i^t\le d_{squeeze}]\tanh(m_i^t).
\]

- Inter-agent repulsion Eq. (32): inverse-distance barrier-like penalty inside the safety band \(d_{col}<d_{ik}<d_{safe}\).
- Hard collision penalty Eq. (33):

\[
r_{i,col}^t=-\mathbf 1(\exists k\ne i:d_{ik}^t\le d_{col}).
\]

- Active-step/energy efficiency penalty Eq. (34):

\[
r_{i,energy}^t=-\mathbf 1[f_i^t=0]\mathbf 1[c_{j_i^t}^t=0].
\]

- Completion Eq. (35):

\[
r_{i,cap}^t=R_{cap}n_{cap}^t+R_{all}\mathbf 1\left[\sum_jc_j^t=N_T\right].
\]

- Total Eq. (36):

\[
r_i^t=w_1r_{i,shell}^t+w_2r_{i,squeeze}^t+w_3r_{i,rep}^t+w_4r_{i,col}^t+w_5r_{i,energy}^t+w_6r_{i,cap}^t.
\]

### Capturability / P0025 comparison
[原文] Sinkhorn guarantees **assignment cardinality feasibility**: one target per AUV and a prescribed number \(q_j\) per target.

[AI判断] It does **not** guarantee **capture feasibility**. The pipeline is:

`learned AUV-target preference → prescribed-capacity feasible assignment → learned subgroup control → terminal spatial-validity test`.

P0025's theory pipeline is:

`coalition winning certificate → feasible coalition-target edge → resource-constrained assignment`.

Therefore P0042 is **not a learned equivalent of P0025**. Its hard constraint says “four AUVs must be assigned,” not “these four AUVs can guarantee capture from this state.” The missing object is a coalition-dependent feasibility/capturability score.

- **No coalition capturability certificate.**
- [AI判断] This leaves genuine method space for \(C(S,j)\) and marginal support value \(\Delta_i=C(S\cup\{i\},j)-C(S,j)\).

## 5. Evidence
- Baselines: [原文] MAPPO without explicit allocation; Greedy Allocation with the same capacity but distance-based assignment; Soft Assignment without Sinkhorn capacity; MLP Encoder replacing allocation-aware GAT.
- Metrics: [原文] reward, all-target success rate (SR), peak capture ratio (PCR), first/all-capture steps, energy/coordination/formation metrics.
- Ablations: [原文] Learned assignment beats greedy distance assignment; hard capacity improves over soft assignment; allocation-aware GAT improves over MLP encoding.
- Simulation: [原文] Base task 8 AUVs / 2 targets, 300-step episodes, 9.6M training steps, 3 random seeds, 1000 evaluation episodes per trained policy.
- Real experiment: [原文] None.
- Scalability/generalization: [原文] Section 4.6 extends from 8 AUVs/2 targets to 12 AUVs/3 targets, with SR changing from 99.5±0.3% to 98.5±0.2% and similar formation quality. The paper calls this a scalability extension. **No explicit “without retraining/zero-shot” statement was found**, unlike TERL; do not infer zero-shot count generalization.

## 6. Limitations
### Author-stated [原文]
- The paper acknowledges simplified environmental/communication conditions; future work points to communication delay/imperfection, incomplete target observations, more complex currents and obstacle-rich settings.

### Evidence-visible [原文 + AI判断]
- **SCREEN CORRECTION:** SCREEN left strict target sensing unresolved. Full text Assumption 1 resolves it: target positions are available to the pursuer team through external support. Therefore this is **not strict local enemy sensing**, despite the actor input being called a local observation.
- Every target is represented in each AUV target feature set, with target prediction and assignment history.
- Pursuers are faster than targets.
- Total available AUVs exactly match total fixed subgroup demand; capacity is prescribed rather than inferred from state/difficulty.
- After capture, assigned subgroup freezes/holds and is not reassigned.
- No repeated/new target arrivals and no persistent search/coverage task.
- No obstacle field or real AUV deployment.
- Target is reactive, not a learned strategic adversary.

### Our inference [AI判断]
- P0042 **kills** broad novelty claims for “learned explicit multi-target assignment + hard capacity + subgroup-conditioned pursuit + geometric capture.”
- It does **not** kill capturability-aware dynamic recruitment under uncertain/local target knowledge, because its allocation feasibility is combinatorial/cardinality-based rather than game-feasibility-based.

## 8. Relation to CoCap
- Closest similarities: multi-agent/multi-target; explicit target-wise allocation; subgroup formation; assignment-conditioned control; CTDE; continuous maneuver actions; meaningful group capture; count-scaling test.
- Fundamental differences: externally supported/global target positions; no persistent search/coverage; fixed capacities/exact supply; no capturability score; no release/reassignment after capture; no repeated arrivals; AUV domain, no obstacles, reactive slower target.
- Does it cover a candidate novelty? **Yes** for explicit learned allocation, hard capacity, subgroup-conditioned control and geometric capture. **No** for local uncertain discovery, capturability-aware dynamic support, coverage opportunity cost, post-capture release/return, repeated arrivals.
- Directly reusable ideas: hard differentiable capacity projection; assignment-conditioned relational re-encoding; separate high-level allocation and low-level control; convex-hull/formation/closing capture diagnostics.
- Non-transferable assumptions: externally supplied target state, exact fixed \(q_j\), exact total supply=demand, faster pursuers, frozen post-capture groups.
- New research questions exposed: Can capacity itself become state-dependent through a learned robust capturability score, and can recruitment/release be optimized against the opportunity cost of removing agents from coverage?
