# READING NOTE — P0036 — OPEN: Online Planning for Multi-UAV Pursuit-Evasion in Unknown Environments Using Deep Reinforcement Learning

## 1. Paper positioning

- Problem: multi-UAV cooperative pursuit of one faster evader in 3-D obstacle environments, with target occlusion, physical quadrotor dynamics, CTBR control, curriculum-style environment generation, and zero-shot transfer to real Crazyflie vehicles.
- Main predecessors: RL/MARL pursuit with point-mass or velocity-level actions; DACOOP/APF-style pursuit; MAPPO; OmniDrones; generic sim-to-real / domain-randomization literature.
- Why this paper was needed: [原文] the authors argue that most RL pursuit work remains at simplified kinematic/high-level-control simulation and fixed scenarios, while real 3-D UAV pursuit requires physical constraints, obstacle avoidance, cooperative behavior, generalization, and a deployable action interface.
- Map role: [AI判断] strongest **CoCap-relevant multi-UAV physical-deployment comparator** in the current READ map: it makes the jump from abstract pursuit actions to calibrated 6-DOF quadrotor training and CTBR hardware execution. Its deployment is nevertheless infrastructure-assisted rather than onboard-perception autonomy.

## 2. Problem setting

- Agents / targets: [原文, Sec. III-A; Sec. V-A] 3 pursuer quadrotors in the reported experiments versus 1 faster evader.
- Single/multi-target: single target only.
- Dynamics: [原文, Sec. III-C] 6-DOF rigid-body quadrotor with state `x=[p,q,v,ω]`, motor-speed first-order response, rotor force/torque models, gravity and attitude coupling.
- Action space: [原文, Sec. IV-A] CTBR = **Collective Thrust and Body Rates**, `a_i=(F, ω_roll, ω_pitch, ω_yaw)`. `F∈[0,1]`; body rates are bounded in `[-π,π] rad/s`. A low-level PID translates CTBR to motor inputs.
- Sensing / observation: [原文, Sec. IV-A] own quaternion orientation and linear velocity; real and predicted relative evader position; relative positions of other quadrotors; relative positions of the `k=3` nearest obstacles.
- Target visibility: [原文, Sec. III-A / Fig. 1] obstacle line-of-sight can block detection. If no quadrotor detects the evader, the real relative evader position is masked by a fixed marker.
- Communication: [原文, Sec. III-A] once detected, a UAV can pass evader information to teammates. The observation therefore does not preserve strictly personal target knowledge after team detection.
- Centralized/decentralized: [原文] formulated as a Dec-POMDP with a parameter-shared policy and trained with MAPPO. [AI判断] decentralized actor execution is supported; the main text does not cleanly expose a separate privileged global-state critic vector, so a stronger claim about exact critic information should not be invented.
- Map / obstacles: [原文, Sec. V-A] 0.9-radius, 1.2-high arena; 4–5 randomly placed cylindrical obstacles (radius 0.1, height 1.2). Adaptive Environment Generator varies obstacle number/position and initial agent/target states.
- Target behavior: [原文, Sec. III-A] explicitly **non-strategic**. Potential-field motion receives repulsion from pursuers, obstacles, and boundaries; speed is constant and direction follows resultant repulsive force.
- Speed ratio: [原文, Sec. V-A] pursuer max speed 1.0; evader nominal speed 1.3. Speed OOD tests also use unseen faster evaders.
- Capture / termination definition: [原文, Sec. III-A; Sec. V-A] capture occurs when the evader enters the capture radius of **any one UAV**; nominal capture radius 0.3; episode max 800 steps. This is not a multi-UAV spatial capture condition and does not require a no-escape enclosure.

## 3. Core idea

### Plain-language summary

OPEN trains a physically executable cooperative pursuit policy rather than a high-level waypoint/velocity-only planner. It combines: (1) a calibrated quadrotor model and CTBR policy interface; (2) an LSTM evader predictor plus attention encoder to continue useful pursuit decisions during occlusion; (3) an adaptive environment generator that spends training effort on medium-difficulty/corner-case tasks while still exploring globally; and (4) two-stage reward refinement that first learns task behavior, then regularizes action smoothness for hardware stability.

### Mathematical / algorithmic mechanism

- [原文, Fig. 2; Sec. IV] MAPPO policy with shared actor, attention-based entity encoder, and evader trajectory prediction.
- [原文, Sec. IV-B] LSTM predicts next `K` evader states from `n` historical steps. Its training input includes positions of **all quadrotors**, evader position/velocity and timestep; evader fields are masked when the target is blocked and undetected by the team.
- [原文, Sec. IV-C, Algorithm 1] Adaptive Environment Generator maintains an active archive of tasks with success rate in `[σ_min,σ_max]=[0.5,0.9]`; local expansion is sampled with `p=0.7` and global random exploration with `0.3`.
- [原文, Sec. IV-D] stage 1 excludes action-smoothness reward; stage 2 restores `exp(-||a_t-a_{t-1}||^2)` with coefficient 2.0 and continues from stage-1 checkpoint.

## 4. Method details

### RL/MARL setup

- Observation: self state + teammate relative positions + `k=3` nearest-obstacle relative positions + evader real relative position if team-visible + predicted future evader trajectory.
- Action: Gaussian CTBR policy.
- Reward: [原文, Sec. IV-A] team capture reward `+6`, distance penalty coefficient `-0.1`, collision penalty `-10`, plus stage-2 smoothness reward.
- Architecture: entity-wise MLP encoders → multi-head self-attention → residual emphasis on self embedding → MLP actor / value head; separate supervised LSTM evader predictor.
- Actor/Critic: actor parameterizes Gaussian action distribution; critic outputs scalar state-value estimate from encoded features.
- CTDE: MAPPO training; shared decentralized policy. Exact privileged critic-state scope is not independently specified enough to infer more than the paper states.
- Replay/on-policy: MAPPO/on-policy; EPN supervised from rollout history.
- Curriculum/exploration: active-archive local expansion + global environment exploration.

### Unknown-environment audit

What is actually “unknown”:

- [原文] obstacle configurations and initial conditions vary across tasks; policy is evaluated on unseen obstacle arrangements/scenarios.
- [原文] the policy receives only nearest-obstacle relative positions rather than the full obstacle set.
- [原文] obstacles can occlude the evader.

What is **not** demonstrated:

- no online SLAM/map construction;
- no learned map belief or occupancy uncertainty;
- no explicit sensor range/FOV model for obstacles;
- no vision/lidar perception pipeline generating obstacle positions;
- no probabilistic target belief retained by the actor beyond masking + learned trajectory prediction.

[AI判断] “unknown environment” here should be normalized as **unseen/variable clutter with partial obstacle-state input and target occlusion**, not `unknown map + onboard mapping + local target belief`.

### Trajectory-prediction audit

- [原文, Sec. IV-B] EPN consumes history of all pursuer positions plus evader position/velocity when available and predicts future evader trajectory.
- [AI判断] It is a useful partial-observability compensator, but not a local Bayesian belief. Its training/input semantics are team-level and exact-state-heavy when observations exist.

## 5. Evidence

### Simulation

- [原文, Sec. V-B/Table I] four test scenarios: Wall, Narrow Gap, Random, Passage. Random and Passage are OOD; Random initially hides the evader; Passage has three escape routes.
- [原文] each numerical experiment averages 300 episodes (100 per seed).
- [原文, Table I] OPEN capture rate: Wall `0.987(0.019)`; Narrow Gap, Random, Passage all `1.000(0.000)`. Collision rates remain low.
- [原文, Fig. 8b] policy is tested across evader speeds not seen in training and remains strong as speed increases.
- [原文, Table II] next-position prediction error remains substantially below capture radius, including Random where target is initially invisible.
- [原文] baseline implementations treat quadrotors as point masses and output velocity commands; this makes the action/dynamics realism gap to OPEN explicit.

### Real experiment / sim-to-real audit

Transfer chain:

`simulation-trained MAPPO+EPN`
→ `calibrated quadrotor dynamics + two-stage smoothness refinement`
→ `real mocap state`
→ `offboard actor/EPN inference`
→ `100 Hz radio CTBR commands`
→ `low-level onboard flight control`
→ `real Crazyflie motion`.

Specifics:

- [原文, Sec. V-F] **3 real Crazyflie 2.1 pursuer UAVs**; max speed 1.0 m/s.
- [原文] external **motion-capture** system provides quadrotor states.
- [原文] target is a **virtual evader**; its true state is inserted into actor/EPN when detection logic says it is visible.
- [原文] actor + EPN run on a **local computer**, not on each UAV; CTBR commands are sent at 100 Hz by radio.
- [原文] four scenario conditions are repeated **5 times** each; Table IV reports mean/std capture steps. The two-stage policy succeeds while the no-smoothness policy crashes/fails in real tests.
- [原文, Fig. 2 / Sec. V-F] physical flight occurs in an obstacle arena, but the paper does not demonstrate onboard obstacle sensing or map estimation.

[AI判断] This proves **zero-shot real-UAV execution of a learned CTBR pursuit policy under external localization/offboard inference**, not a fully onboard perception-localization-decision stack and not pursuit of a physical evader.

### Deployment-realism layer

Using the R-LM-02 ladder:

1. kinematic simulation — exceeded;
2. vehicle-dynamic simulation — **yes**;
3. noise/delay/actuator realism — **partial** (calibrated dynamics/motor response + action smoothing; perception uncertainty/delay is not a central model);
4. SITL/HITL — not the headline evidence;
5. external-localization real UAV — **yes** (with offboard inference and virtual target);
6. onboard-perception real UAV — **no**.

## 6. Limitations

### Author-stated [原文]

- policy can support varying drone counts but requires retraining for good performance;
- future work targets vision-based methods for unstructured environments.

### Evidence-visible [原文 + AI判断]

1. single target; no target-wise assignment;
2. evader is heuristic/non-strategic;
3. capture is any-one proximity, not multi-UAV encirclement/no-escape;
4. once any pursuer detects target, target information can be shared team-wide;
5. obstacle input is exact relative position of nearest obstacles rather than demonstrated onboard perception;
6. real target is virtual;
7. real state comes from mocap and inference is offboard;
8. hardware repetitions are small (`5` per scenario) and Table IV focuses capture steps rather than a large-statistics deployment success study;
9. episode ends at capture; no post-capture mission lifecycle.

### Our inference [AI判断]

P0036’s main frontier contribution is **physical policy synthesis/deployment**, not strategic opponent reasoning, resource allocation, coalition feasibility, or lifecycle. It kills weak novelty wording around CTBR/real-UAV/sim-to-real/trajectory-prediction-assisted pursuit, but it does not close the current CoCap intersection.

## 8. Relation to CoCap

### Closest similarities

- multi-UAV cooperative pursuit;
- occlusion-sensitive target visibility;
- obstacle interaction;
- continuous body-level control;
- policy generalization across environment variants;
- realistic deployment concerns.

### Fundamental differences

- one target vs CoCap multi-target/lifecycle goal;
- all three agents are permanent pursuers rather than a fungible coverage pool;
- no target-wise allocation/support recruitment;
- no coalition-size decision;
- no capturability certificate/estimate;
- heuristic non-strategic evader;
- no post-capture release/coverage recovery/repeated arrival;
- team-wide target sharing after detection;
- hardware uses virtual evader, mocap and offboard inference.

### Does it cover a candidate novelty?

- real-UAV pursuit: **yes; standalone claim dead**.
- zero-shot sim-to-real pursuit: **yes in infrastructure-assisted form; standalone claim dead**.
- continuous/physical CTBR control: **yes; standalone claim dead**.
- unknown-clutter pursuit: **yes in the paper’s variable/unseen-layout sense**; do not claim novelty from that alone.
- trajectory-prediction-assisted pursuit: **yes; standalone claim dead**.
- local/intermittent information + capturability + same-pool lifecycle coupling: **no**.

### Directly reusable ideas

- CTBR as a deployment-oriented action interface;
- calibrating vehicle dynamics before hardware transfer;
- action-smoothness continuation/refinement;
- active archive / corner-case environment curriculum;
- prediction error and OOD speed/layout testing as diagnostics.

### Non-transferable assumptions

- virtual target/mocap/offboard state infrastructure;
- exact nearest-obstacle positions;
- shared target coordinate after detection;
- fixed all-agent pursuit team;
- any-one proximity capture.

### New research questions exposed

- if CoCap stays simulation-only, what action/dynamics/noise level is sufficient to avoid looking physically under-modeled relative to OPEN?
- if `C(S,j)` is learned, should its training distribution include vehicle-dynamic/action delay perturbations rather than ideal kinematics?
