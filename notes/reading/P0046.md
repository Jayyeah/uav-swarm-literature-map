# READING NOTE — P0046 — Coordination in Human-Agent-Robot Teamwork

## 1. Paper positioning
- Problem: policy-governed coordination in mixed human-agent-robot teams, illustrated by a Navy-pier field exercise in which teams secure boundaries, search for an intruder, dynamically modify team membership, and successfully apprehend the intruder.
- Main predecessors: KAoS policy services, teamwork-centered autonomy, adjustable autonomy, human-agent joint-activity theory.
- Why this paper was needed: [原文] autonomous components need regulatory structures, common ground, directability and progress/completion notification to function as team members under changing roles and plans.
- R-LIFE-01 role: [AI判断] historical novelty-boundary anchor for **apprehension completion → post-success successor behavior including “resume patrolling.”** It is not a modern autonomous swarm coalition algorithm, but it kills a broad historical claim that post-capture return-to-patrol is unprecedented.

## 2. Problem setting
- Agents / targets: [原文] two humans and five ground robots; one intruder in the demonstrated Navy-pier scenario.
- Dynamics: heterogeneous physical robots under autonomous and human-directed behaviors; no unified pursuit dynamics model.
- Action space: high-level autonomous search/boundary-monitoring/following plus human language/teleoperation commands.
- Sensing / observation: robots use combinations of sonar, GPS, cameras and laser range finders; image processing supports detection/tracking.
- Communication: wireless routers plus KAoS messaging, directory and policy services.
- Centralized/decentralized: mixed/hierarchical. A human Commander forms teams and authorizes team changes; robot leaders and autonomous robot behaviors execute portions of the mission.
- Map / obstacles: cluttered Navy pier.
- Target behavior: intruder hiding in the pier scenario; no formal adversarial dynamics.
- Speed ratio: not formalized.
- Capture / termination definition: [原文] **successful apprehension** of the intruder. No geometric/no-escape capture theorem is claimed.

## 3. Core idea

### Plain-language summary
The paper is primarily about coordination infrastructure, not pursuit control. Team membership and roles can be created and modified dynamically; policies govern who may command whom and require team members to notify others when a task has completed, failed, or been aborted. In the field exercise, a robot detects the intruder, the Lieutenant requests another robot that is outside his team, the Commander dynamically reassigns that robot, and the apprehension succeeds.

The lifecycle-relevant statement appears immediately afterward in the policy discussion: once an intruder has been apprehended, all human and robot team members are notified, and each robot begins the appropriate post-success task it was designed for, with examples including **“return to base” and “resume patrolling.”**

### Mathematical / algorithmic mechanism
There is no coalition-optimization objective analogous to P0044. KAoS supplies:
- dynamic organization/team/role management;
- authorization policies and chain of command;
- obligation policies for acknowledgement, progress appraisal and completion notification;
- successor behavior triggered by a shared understanding that the team goal is finished.

[AI判断] The relevant mechanism is therefore **policy-triggered workflow continuation**, not automatic distributed capture-coalition dissolution.

## 4. Method details

### Lifecycle state-machine audit

#### State 0 — Nominal mission
- [原文, Sec. 5.3] the Commander first secures two escape boundaries, then directs the two teams to **search the area**.
- [原文, Sec. 3.5] available robot behaviors include autonomous area search and boundary monitoring.
- [AI判断] The executed field scenario is best normalized as `boundary security → area search`, not a persistent quantitative coverage controller. The later phrase “resume patrolling” is a possible successor task, not proof that the exact pre-apprehension state in this field run was a steady patrol state.

#### State 1 — Trigger
- [原文, Sec. 5.3] **when the intruder is found by a robot**, the appropriate team leader is informed under the coordination policies.
- This is a real field detection event, not an externally released abstract task.

#### State 2 — Recruitment / coalition formation
- [原文, Sec. 3.4] the directory/policy services allow dynamic team formation and modification.
- [原文, Sec. 5.3] the Lieutenant initially cannot use the tBot because it is not in his team; after permission is requested, the Commander **dynamically assigns the tBot to the Lieutenant’s team**.
- [AI判断] This is real dynamic membership/recruitment, but it is human-chain-of-command mediated. It is not autonomous target-specific coalition optimization, adaptive cardinality, or fungible swarm borrowing.

#### State 3 — Capture / completion
- [原文, Sec. 5.3] after the tBot is added, “the apprehension was successful.”
- [原文, Sec. 6.2] the policy logic explicitly treats “once an intruder has been apprehended” as successful completion of the team goal.
- [AI判断] “Apprehension” is semantically stronger than generic task completion but does not provide PE capture geometry or a strategy guarantee.

#### State 4 — Release
- [原文, Sec. 6.2] a completion-notification obligation fires when a robot learns the goal is achieved; all teams are informed.
- [AI判断] The paper does **not** explicitly say that the apprehension team/coalition is dissolved at that instant. The transition is a team-goal completion broadcast that enables each robot’s next behavior.

#### State 5 — Return
- [原文, Sec. 6.2] following successful goal completion, each robot begins the appropriate task it was designed to perform, examples: **“return to base, resume patrolling.”**
- [AI判断] This is strong evidence for a **policy/workflow-level post-apprehension return-to-patrol precedent**.
- However, it is **not** strong evidence that:
  1. every robot resumes patrol;
  2. the specific robot recruited for apprehension is shown physically returning to patrol;
  3. a patrol formation/coverage metric is restored;
  4. the field-exercise narrative actually records the post-apprehension trajectory.
- The text uses `e.g.`: successor task is role-dependent.

#### State 6 — Repetition
- [原文] the demonstrated mission has one intruder/apprehension.
- [AI判断] no repeated-intruder arrival process or repeated patrol→apprehend→patrol cycle is established.

### Evidence-strength judgment for “resume patrolling”
The paper supports three different statements at different strengths:

1. **Strong [原文]:** the field exercise actually reaches successful intruder apprehension.
2. **Strong [原文]:** the implemented teamwork policy notifies all teams upon successful completion.
3. **Moderate-to-strong [原文 + AI判断]:** the architecture’s intended/implemented successor behavior may include `resume patrolling`; this is given as an example in the field-experiment policy discussion, but the paper does not trace a particular capturer through a measured patrol resumption.

Therefore the historically safe wording is:

> **“Post-apprehension return/resumption of patrol has a prior policy/workflow precedent.”**

The unsafe stronger wording would be:

> “Bradshaw et al. experimentally demonstrated the same capturing robot autonomously restoring a patrol coverage state after capture.”

The second is not supported by this paper.

## 5. Evidence
- Field experiment: [原文] Navy-pier exercise with 2 humans + 5 robots; real detection/search and successful apprehension.
- Dynamic modification: [原文] tBot is reassigned through the Commander’s chain of command during the mission.
- Policy enforcement: [原文] attempted unauthorized use of tBot is blocked; reassignment changes permissions/obligations automatically.
- Completion handling: [原文] apprehension triggers notification and successor-task logic.
- Coverage/patrol recovery metric: none.
- Repeated arrivals: none.
- Visual evidence: Fig. 4 shows the initial hierarchy; Fig. 5 shows a three-tier team after reorganization. The paper does not provide a post-apprehension patrol-restoration figure or metric.

## 6. Limitations

### Author-stated [原文]
The paper focuses on coordination/policy infrastructure and uses the field exercise to explore these capabilities rather than solve pursuit/control optimally.

### Evidence-visible [原文 + AI判断]
1. Human Commander has decisive authority over team formation and recruitment.
2. No automatic capture-coalition optimization or adaptive coalition-size rule.
3. “Resume patrolling” is successor-policy wording, not a robot-identity trace with restored coverage measurement.
4. Only one intruder is demonstrated.
5. No formal capture or no-escape semantics.
6. Nominal field sequence is boundary security and area search rather than a persistent coverage objective.

### Our inference [AI判断]
P0046 should be used as a **novelty correction**, not as a direct algorithmic competitor. It means CoCap must never claim “first post-capture return to patrol.” CoCap can still claim a narrower autonomous-swarm lifecycle if it demonstrates explicit fungible same-agent recovery and repeatability without human task reassignment.

## 8. Relation to CoCap
- Closest similarities: event detection; dynamic addition of support; explicit successful apprehension; completion notification; successor mission behavior including patrol.
- Fundamental differences: human/policy-mediated hierarchy, small heterogeneous human-robot team, no fungible swarm pool, no repeated arrivals, no coverage restoration metric, no capturability-aware recruitment.
- Does it cover a candidate novelty? **Yes** for the isolated historical notion `apprehension complete → resume patrolling`; **no** for the autonomous same-pool repeated lifecycle.
- Directly reusable ideas: completion should be a first-class event broadcast; successor behavior should be explicit rather than implicit; team role change should automatically carry the correct rights/state.
- Non-transferable assumptions: human Commander authorization and rich policy infrastructure.
- New research questions exposed: after CoCap capture, is “return to coverage” a defined state transition with an observable recovery criterion, or merely an absence of capture reward?
