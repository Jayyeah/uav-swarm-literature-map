# READING NOTE — P0040 — AgilePE: Autonomous UAV Pursuit-Evasion via Self-Play Reinforcement Learning

## 1. Paper positioning

- Problem: 1v1 adversarial aerial pursuit-evasion with learned pursuer and learned evader, direct CTBR control, historical-policy self-play, and zero-shot physical deployment.
- Main predecessors: naive self-play; Fictitious Self-Play (FSP); Prioritized Fictitious Self-Play (PFSP); MAPPO; agile quadrotor CTBR control/sim-to-real work; recent multi-drone competitive-learning systems such as VolleyBots/HCSP.
- Why this paper was needed: [原文] the authors target three gaps together: high-level/kinematic action interfaces, instability/catastrophic forgetting in adversarial co-evolution, and sim-to-real failure of aggressive learned flight policies.
- Map role: [AI判断] strongest current **strategic-opponent competitor** in the CoCap literature map. Its contribution is opponent-distribution training and physically agile adversarial execution, not swarm allocation or lifecycle closure.

### Publication status audit

- [原文] uploaded manuscript is arXiv `2608.14135v1`, dated 2026-08-14.
- [Web核验, 2026-09-16] arXiv metadata lists `8 pages, 7 figures. Under review`; no formal peer-reviewed venue/DOI publication was verified.
- [AI判断] Treat as a frontier **preprint**, not evidence-weight-equivalent to formal RA-L/ICRA/TRO publications. Its technical results are still important for boundary mapping.

## 2. Problem setting

- Agents / targets: [原文, Sec. III-A] 1 pursuer vs 1 evader.
- Multi-target: no.
- Game: [原文] partially observable zero-sum 1v1 PE in a bounded 3-D workspace.
- Sim workspace: [原文] 6 m × 6 m × 3 m, 2048 parallel arenas.
- Dynamics: [原文, Sec. III-A/B/D] 6-DOF state representation with CTBR interface. The deployment simulator uses deterministic RK4 state integration plus calibrated command-response latency/noise rather than relying on Isaac Sim’s internal force/contact solver.
- Action space: [原文] `u=[T,ω_x,ω_y,ω_z]^T`, collective thrust plus desired body rates.
- Sensing / observation: [原文, Sec. III-A] own position, attitude, velocity and recent CTBR commands; temporal window of opponent state; opponent entries masked when outside conical FOV or occluded.
- Target visibility: simulated frustum test + cylinder occlusion test; missing entries replaced with mask value.
- Communication: no teammate communication problem in 1v1. Real deployment still depends on mocap streaming for state estimation.
- Target behavior: both pursuer and evader are learned during adversarial training.
- Capture / termination definition: [原文, Sec. III-A/B] pursuer reward encourages keeping evader in FOV and reducing distance; episode terminates on timeout, out-of-bounds, or inter-agent collision below a safety threshold. [AI判断] the paper’s operational metric is primarily **tracking/in-FOV adversarial performance**, not a clean multi-UAV terminal capture semantics. Later text sometimes says “win/capture rate,” but the main quantitative tables are labeled In-FOV Rate.
- Obstacles: task formulation supports cylinder occlusion; however [原文, Sec. VII] reported experiments are restricted to **1v1 obstacle-free environments**.

## 3. Core idea

### Plain-language summary

AgilePE makes both sides learn and prevents the training process from overfitting to only the current opponent. It first shows that current-policy self-play can cycle/collapse, then archives older pursuer/evader checkpoints and trains against them. FSP samples old opponents uniformly; PFSP gives more probability to opponents on which the current policy performs poorly, creating a moving hard-opponent curriculum. The resulting policies directly output CTBR commands and are deployed on real quadrotors after adding actuator latency/noise and domain randomization in simulation.

### Mathematical / algorithmic mechanism

- [原文, Sec. III-C] base learner is MAPPO.
- Naive SP: latest pursuer and evader face each other and update synchronously; no historical opponent overhead.
- FSP: maintains separate historical policy pools for pursuer and evader. During round `k`, a learner faces a frozen historical opponent sampled uniformly from the opponent pool, excluding the current policy; newly optimized policy is archived after the round.
- [原文, Fig. 2 / Sec. III-C] environments are partitioned so pursuit and evasion learning can proceed bilaterally against historical opponents in the same training stage.
- PFSP: replaces uniform opponent sampling with priority based on the current policy’s **failure rate** against each historical candidate, focusing training on bottleneck opponents.
- [AI判断] the paper contains an internal wording inconsistency: one summary sentence describes who is prioritized in a way that can read opposite to “failure-rate proportional” sampling. The explicit Sec. IV-A3 definition should control interpretation: **hard historical opponents receive higher priority**.

## 4. Method details

### Opponent training audit

#### Are both sides learned?

- [原文] yes. Pursuer and evader have current policies and historical pools.

#### Simultaneous vs alternating?

- Naive SP: [原文] current-vs-current and synchronous updates.
- FSP/PFSP: [原文] bilateral training uses separate environment partitions where each current learner trains against frozen historical policies from the opposing pool. [AI判断] this is best described as **parallel bilateral round-based learning against historical opponents**, not simply “one fixed learned evader” and not pure current-current self-play.

#### Historical pool?

- [原文, Sec. IV-A2] yes, for both sides: `H={π_0,...,π_{k-1}}`; new checkpoints are appended after each round.

#### PFSP sampling score?

- [原文, Sec. IV-A3] probability is proportional to the current agent’s failure rate against the candidate historical policy; initial win/capture-style performance is used to build the priority.

#### Current vs historical mixture?

- [原文] naive SP is current-current. FSP/PFSP descriptions focus on sampled historical opponents; the paper does not provide a clean scalar “x% current / y% historical” mixture for PFSP. Do not invent one.

#### Forgetting prevention?

- [原文] historical pools broaden the opponent distribution; FSP/PFSP are motivated explicitly as preventing strategy collapse/catastrophic forgetting.
- [原文, Fig. 5] cross-evaluation matrices over archived policies are used as evidence of evolutionary progress and retention.

#### Curriculum / initialization?

- [原文] overall pipeline mentions bootstrapped warm-up and multi-dimensional curriculum before hierarchical adversarial training.
- [AI判断] the 8-page manuscript does not specify enough schedule/initial-opponent detail to reconstruct these components precisely. They should not be promoted beyond what is documented.

### Observation / information audit

- Sim policy receives exact state variables when visible, with software masking outside FOV/occlusion.
- Own position is part of the observation, so global/state-estimation infrastructure is assumed.
- 1v1 avoids teammate-information/communication/resource-allocation complexity.
- [AI判断] strategic sophistication is therefore learned under a stronger state-based sensing setup than onboard local-vision pursuit: the hard uncertainty is opponent strategy and intermittent visibility, not raw perception/localization.

### Dynamics / action / safety

- [原文, Sec. III-D/Table I] outer policy step `Δt=0.016 s` (~60 Hz); RK4 inner step `0.0016 s`, 10 substeps.
- nominal thrust response delay 45 ms; body-rate delay 30 ms.
- multiplicative noise: thrust ±10%, body rates ±30%.
- delay jitter: thrust ±30%, body-rate ±20%.
- reward also penalizes overspeed, boundary proximity, inter-UAV close approach, and command roughness.
- [AI判断] compared with P0036, AgilePE models response delay/noise more explicitly but uses a deterministic hardware-aligned integration backend rather than P0036’s motor-level rigid-body simulator. Neither paper simply dominates the other on every physical-model axis.

## 5. Evidence

### Strategic robustness

- [原文, Fig. 4] naive self-play is run with multiple random seeds; late training often shows unilateral evader collapse/high in-FOV rate rather than robust co-evolution.
- [原文, Fig. 5] FSP and PFSP use archived-policy cross-evaluation matrices. Later policies are evaluated across earlier opponents.
- [原文, Sec. IV-A3] PFSP Predator Iter. 6 achieves >0.82 in-FOV rate against all historical Prey versions in the shown matrix.
- [原文, Table II] policies are also evaluated against scripted opponents.
- [原文, Sec. V] case studies include a rigid direct-tracking pursuer and a heterogeneous “fast-spinning” evader, eliciting dash/flank and standoff tracking behaviors.

What the paper **does not** establish:

- no best-response/exploitability computation;
- no Nash gap or formal equilibrium result;
- no independent unseen learned-policy suite from another training lineage;
- no human-controlled opponent evaluation;
- no broad multi-seed PFSP generalization table;
- no population-level statistical guarantee.

[AI判断] Evidence supports **reduced forgetting and robustness across the paper’s historical-opponent pool plus selected scripted/heterogeneous opponents**. It does not justify the stronger statement “game-theoretically optimal” or “robust to arbitrary unseen strategic policies.”

### Metric ambiguity

- [原文] Table II is titled **In-FOV Rate**.
- [原文] surrounding prose sometimes interprets increasing/decreasing entries as “survival,” “win,” or “capture” in a way that is not always directionally transparent.
- [AI判断] use the matrices as relative cross-play evidence, but do not silently convert every entry into a calibrated capture probability.

### Real-world deployment audit

Transfer chain:

`PFSP/self-play-trained CTBR policy`
→ `hardware-aligned latency/noise simulation`
→ `external mocap + onboard IMU state estimate`
→ `onboard Jetson Orin NX inference at 60 Hz`
→ `CTBR commands`
→ `onboard PX4 flight controller`
→ `two physical UAVs interacting`.

Specifics:

- [原文, Sec. VI / Fig. 7] physical 1v1: one real pursuer and one real evader quadrotor.
- [原文] policy inference runs **onboard** a Jetson Orin NX at 60 Hz.
- [原文] CTBR commands are executed by onboard PX4 Autopilot.
- [原文] external motion capture supplies pose at 120 Hz and is fused with onboard IMU.
- [原文] body-rate response is tuned to <20 ms in hardware.
- [原文] real snapshots demonstrate dash-and-flank and lateral-oscillation evasion behaviors.
- [原文, Sec. VII] experiments are 1v1 and obstacle-free; state-based observations remain a limitation and vision is future work.
- [AI判断] the real section is primarily **qualitative behavior validation**: it does not report a hardware trial count, capture/success rate, confidence intervals, or an opponent cross-play matrix on hardware.

### Deployment-realism layer

Using the R-LM-02 ladder:

1. kinematic simulation — exceeded;
2. vehicle-dynamic simulation — **yes/qualified** via 6-DOF/CTBR state propagation;
3. noisy/delayed sensing + actuator abstraction — **yes for actuator response/noise; state sensing still mocap-based**;
4. SITL/HITL — not the headline result;
5. external-localization real UAV — **yes**, with **onboard inference** and a physical opponent;
6. onboard-perception real UAV — **no**.

## 6. Limitations

### Author-stated [原文, Sec. VII]

- only 1v1 engagements;
- reported experiments obstacle-free;
- state-based observations; vision-based perception is future work;
- multi-agent coordination/cluttered terrain left for future work.

### Evidence-visible [原文 + AI判断]

1. preprint / under-review status;
2. no multi-UAV coalition or multi-target assignment;
3. no persistent nominal mission/lifecycle;
4. capture semantics/primary metric are weaker and less clean than CoCap’s terminal multi-agent capture goal;
5. external mocap remains essential despite onboard policy compute;
6. physical validation lacks repetition statistics and quantitative robustness matrix;
7. strategic generalization is evaluated mainly within historical pools plus scripted/heterogeneous cases, not independently trained unseen learned opponents.

### Our inference [AI判断]

P0040 is a very strong warning against weak “strategic evader” claims. However, its sophistication is orthogonal to CoCap’s current resource-allocation/lifecycle novelty: it solves a hard **1v1 policy co-evolution + agile control** problem, not a same-pool multi-target capture/coverage problem.

## 7. Literature lineage

- Important predecessors: naive SP → FSP → PFSP; MAPPO; generic sim-to-real and CTBR agile flight.
- Same-era alternatives: P0036 is stronger on multi-UAV/obstacle quantitative hardware comparison but has a heuristic/virtual target; P0033 has learned competitors in swarm pursuit but no historical-opponent population machinery.
- Named methodology chain from paper: VolleyBots (NeurIPS 2025) and hierarchical co-self-play multi-drone volleyball (CoRL 2025) show population/hierarchical adversarial learning in aerial multi-agent sports. [AI判断] these are methodology lineage, not evidence that the same PE boundary was already closed before AgilePE.

## 8. Relation to CoCap

### Closest similarities

- learned strategic target;
- intermittent FOV visibility in simulation;
- continuous physical UAV action;
- zero-shot physical deployment;
- policy robustness against changing opponents.

### Fundamental differences

- 1v1 vs multi-UAV/multi-target;
- no coalition, support, target assignment, or adaptive subgroup size;
- no nominal coverage/search duty;
- no capture→release→coverage recovery;
- no repeated arrivals;
- real sensing depends on external mocap and state observations;
- reported real validation is obstacle-free.

### Does it cover a candidate novelty?

- learned strategic evader: **yes; standalone claim dead**.
- self-play pursuit: **yes; standalone claim dead**.
- historical-opponent/FSP/PFSP pursuit: **yes at preprint evidence level; cannot be a CoCap headline novelty**.
- physical bilateral UAV adversarial execution: **yes, but with mocap and qualitative validation**.
- robust pursuit against arbitrary opponent distributions: **only partially**. Historical-pool robustness is shown; unseen independently learned-policy robustness/exploitability is not.
- information + capturability + lifecycle coupling: **no**.

### Capturability bridge audit

- [AI判断] neither the MAPPO critic nor PFSP win/in-FOV statistics are a coalition capturability object `C(S,j)`.
- 1v1 has no coalition cardinality or marginal pursuer value.
- A legitimate design lesson is only conceptual: if CoCap learns `C(S,j)`, it may need to define capturability over an **opponent distribution** rather than one scripted evader, e.g. `C_Π(S,j)=P(capture | S, belief, π_e~Π)`. This is a CoCap design inference, not an AgilePE contribution.

### Directly reusable ideas

- historical opponent pools and cross-play matrices;
- PFSP/hard-opponent sampling if strategic robustness becomes a training requirement;
- held-out opponent evaluation protocol;
- explicit actuator latency/noise modeling;
- onboard inference architecture as a deployment reference.

### Non-transferable assumptions

- 1v1 zero-sum structure;
- exact state variables from mocap/state estimator;
- no resource allocation/lifecycle burden;
- obstacle-free reported physical experiments;
- weakly specified terminal capture semantics relative to multi-UAV capture.
