## Executive result

[AI判断] The lifecycle gap **PARTIALLY SURVIVES**.

The SCREEN closes almost every weak, edge-level novelty claim:

- successful apprehension followed by a post-success policy that includes `resume patrolling` already exists (P0046);
- completion-triggered temporary coalition dissolution and reusable resources already exist (P0043, and generically P0045);
- actual capture followed by continued reuse/reassignment of the capturing resource pool exists (P0044);
- the same homogeneous agents can cycle `patrol → local pursuit → patrol` under repeated intruders (P0048), although the return trigger is target exit/loss rather than capture;
- persistent patrol under repeated stochastic alerts (P0050) and online defense against arbitrary repeated intruders with true capture/neutralization (P0051) are both established.

[AI判断] However, none of the nine papers closes the full same-pool intersection:

`persistent area coverage/search`
→ `target-triggered temporary capture coalition`
→ `terminal capture/completion`
→ `completion-triggered coalition/resource release`
→ `the same agents restore nominal area coverage/search`
→ `repeat under new arrivals`.

The surviving candidate gap is therefore **integration of these primitives in one fungible resource pool**, not any isolated lifecycle transition.

# Per-paper screening notes

## P0043 — Liu 2025

- [原文] UAV mission states include `uncommitted`, `committed`, and `sticky`; an uncommitted UAV performs a **sneak-like move in the mission space with no assigned target**. This controller combines collision avoidance, target attraction and boundary handling. It is not area coverage or patrol.
- [原文] Sticky UAVs at a target form a **temporary combat coalition**, conduct a synchronized strike, after which **the coalition dissolves and each UAV returns to the uncommitted state**.
- [原文] In the simulation, when a target is destroyed, a **pop-up target** of the same type is randomly generated, maintaining target workload across successive kills.
- [AI判断] This is the strongest retained proof that `completion → explicit coalition dissolution → reusable pool → repeated arrivals` is already anteceded. It does **not** show `return-to-coverage`: `uncommitted ≠ coverage`.
- **Decision: MUST READ.** It directly closes a key lifecycle edge and must delimit any resource-release novelty claim.

## P0044 — Chen, Tang & Guo 2024, KS-COAL

- [原文] The heterogeneous system has **fixed SCOUT and SWAT capability roles**: SCOUTs explore/detect; SWATs capture target robots or defend/encircle resources. Therefore the system is not one fungible pool cycling between exploration and capture.
- [原文] Capture coalitions are formed online among SWAT robots; a target is captured when it satisfies the task's capture geometry and then becomes **immobile**.
- [原文] Hardware scenarios show concrete post-capture reuse: after target 0 is captured, the same SWAT robots switch to capture target 1; after target 1 is captured, SWAT robots return to resource-encirclement/defense tasks.
- [原文] SCOUT target positions are shared to SWAT robots. Communication assumptions are substantially stronger than CoCap-style unresolved local belief; SCOUT and SWAT teams use different communication assumptions.
- [原文] The large simulation begins with many targets; captured targets are removed. **New resources are continually generated**, but SCREEN did not find an exogenous process generating new target robots. Thus many captures are not equivalent to repeated target arrivals.
- [AI判断] This is the **strongest capture-specific lifecycle competitor**: it combines exploration, dynamic capture coalitions, real terminal capture, post-capture SWAT reuse and physical robot evidence. Its decisive gaps are fixed roles and return-to-resource-defense rather than same-agent return-to-exploration/coverage.
- **Decision: MUST READ.** This is the most direct threat to a loosely worded CoCap capture/coalition/reuse claim.

## P0045 — HECTOR 2026

- [原文] Missions are released online at random times; experiments include delivery, surveillance and dynamic-capture tasks.
- [原文] Dynamic coalition formation repeatedly builds subteams; **coalitions dissolve upon subtask completion and robots are reassigned**, with worked examples of robots finishing one joint subtask then splitting to different later subtasks.
- [原文] Moving-target positions are assumed available in real time through external sensing/tracking infrastructure.
- [原文] A dynamic `capture` mission is represented through moving subtasks completed by specified actions such as delivery/grasping. This is task completion, not a PE/no-escape or immobilization guarantee.
- [AI判断] HECTOR proves that generic `completion→dissolve→reassign` under continual arrivals is not new, but `generic reassignment ≠ return-to-coverage` and `task called capture ≠ terminal capture`.
- **Decision: MAP.** Its lifecycle primitive is important but is structurally redundant with the more task-specific P0043/P0044 for the minimal READ set.

## P0046 — Bradshaw et al. 2008

- [原文] The Navy-pier field exercise explicitly reports an intruder being discovered and **successfully apprehended** by a mixed human-agent-robot team.
- [原文] Team membership/roles can be dynamically modified under policy and chain-of-command authorization; the demonstration includes dynamically assigning another robot to help the apprehension task.
- [原文] In the paper's field-experiment discussion, once the intruder is apprehended all teams are notified and **each robot begins an appropriate post-success task**, with examples including `return to base` and **`resume patrolling`**.
- [AI判断] The evidence is strong enough to kill a standalone novelty claim of “post-capture/apprehension return to patrol.” The caveat is that this is a policy-governed mixed human/robot architecture: the paper does not trace one specific capturer through a quantitatively measured patrol-restoration trajectory, nor show repeated intruders.
- **Decision: MUST READ.** This is the historical novelty-boundary anchor for capture completion→post-success patrol behavior.

## P0047 — Paley, Techy & Woolsey 2009

- [原文] UAVs maintain coordinated perimeter patrol; when an alert occurs, a nearby UAV leaves the patrol, follows a minimum-time response path, and **after passing over the alert site resumes coordinated patrol**.
- [AI判断] This is an unusually clean same-agent `nominal patrol → event response → nominal patrol` predecessor.
- [AI判断] It is **not** a capture predecessor: servicing/passing over an alert location does not imply interception, immobilization or apprehension, and no temporary coalition is formed.
- **Decision: MAP.** Useful lineage support, but P0046/P0048 cover more decisive lifecycle boundaries for READ.

## P0048 — Duarte, Oliveira & Christensen 2014

- [原文] A large homogeneous aquatic-drone swarm patrols a long strip using local sensing and communication. A local camera detection activates pursuit and nearby agents can join through local alerts.
- [原文] Pursuer count is adaptive: if too many drones pursue, some cease pursuit, typically retaining two for triangulation.
- [原文] The **same drones revert to Patrol** when the intruder exits the patrol zone or when a pursuer has not seen the intruder for five minutes; nonparticipants continue patrolling.
- [原文] The long simulation injects repeated intruder crossings—44 intruders over 24 hours, with recurring crossings after the initial period.
- [AI判断] This is the closest retained **same-pool persistent lifecycle near miss**. It already has persistent nominal patrol, local detection, temporary/dynamic pursuit subgroup, same-agent recovery and repeated arrivals. The missing edge is decisive: recovery is triggered by target exit/loss, **not terminal capture**.
- **Decision: MUST READ.** It defines exactly how narrow the surviving integration gap is.

## P0049 — Wright et al. 2008

- [原文] The behavior named `Capture` commands a robot to maintain a fixed distance from the intruder; in the 1-D Lego prototype a touch sensor puts the robot into `Capture` and it remains in contact with a block-of-wood intruder.
- [原文] Some state-machine branches return to Patrol after escape, hitting the perimeter or a timer. These are not successful-capture completion transitions.
- [原文] In the prototype's nominal successful-capture condition, **both capturers remain at the intruder location until further instructions**, reducing the portion of the boundary actively patrolled.
- [AI判断] This directly resolves the SEARCH ambiguity: `Capture` is a state-name false positive, not a demonstrated terminal `capture→release→patrol` lifecycle.
- **Decision: ARCHIVE.** Preserve as a terminology/false-positive caution; no READ slot is justified.

## P0050 — Chandler et al. 2009

- [Web核验] The AIAA paper concerns perimeter patrol with randomly occurring alerts; false alarms are modeled as a **Poisson process**, and the abstract reports a base-defense flight test.
- [原文/abstract] The UAV responds to an alert site and loiters while a human determines whether the alert is nuisance or an actual threat; the optimization chooses service/dwell time.
- [AI判断] `Poisson alert arrivals ≠ repeated captured threats`, and `alert servicing ≠ capture`.
- **NEEDS_FULLTEXT:** primary 2009 full text was not available in this SCREEN for an exact quotation proving the post-service same-UAV return and patrol-formation recovery. Later same-lineage material describes servicing before resuming patrol, but this should not be silently back-projected into the 2009 paper.
- **Decision: MAP.** The unresolved detail cannot affect the lifecycle-gap verdict because capture itself is absent.

## P0051 — Bajaj et al. 2023

- [原文] An arbitrary number of intruders may be released at arbitrary times and locations; the online input is progressively revealed and intruder positions are known after release.
- [原文] A fixed heterogeneous defender pair—a stationary turret and a mobile vehicle—captures/neutralizes intruders. A captured intruder is removed; neutralization has no extra service time.
- [AI判断] This is strong evidence that **true terminal capture under repeated online arrivals** is already an established defense problem, but it has no temporary capture coalition, coalition dissolution or same-agent return to patrol. The defenders simply continue the online defense policy.
- **Decision: MAP.** Important repeated-capture lineage anchor, not a same-pool lifecycle competitor.

# False-positive corrections

1. **P0043:** `uncommitted` is sneak-like no-target movement; it is **not** coverage/search. Explicit coalition dissolution therefore does not establish coverage restoration.
2. **P0044:** SCOUT and SWAT are fixed heterogeneous roles. SWAT capture→resource-defense reuse is real, but **fixed scouts + fixed pursuers ≠ same fungible resource cycling**. Many captured targets also do not prove repeated target arrivals.
3. **P0045:** `capture` is a mission/task label implemented through generic task actions; **generic reassignment ≠ return-to-coverage**.
4. **P0046:** `resume patrolling` is an explicit post-success policy behavior in a real exercise architecture, but the paper does not prove every capturer independently returns to a restored coverage formation.
5. **P0047:** alert response/flyover is **not target capture**.
6. **P0048:** same-agent return is strong, but it is driven by intruder exit/loss timeout, **not capture completion**.
7. **P0049:** a state named `Capture` is **not terminal capture**; the prototype's capturers actually stay with the intruder until further instructions.
8. **P0050:** repeated Poisson alerts/false alarms are **not repeated terminal captures**.
9. **P0051:** capture under repeated arrivals exists, but continued defense is not an explicit `coalition dissolve→return-to-patrol` lifecycle.

# Nine core questions

## 1. Is isolated `post-capture return to patrol` already clearly anteceded?

**Yes, with a scope caveat.**

- [原文] P0046 reports successful intruder apprehension in a field exercise and defines the immediate post-success policy so each robot begins an appropriate successor behavior, including `resume patrolling`.
- [AI判断] Therefore **standalone post-capture-return novelty is dead**. The stronger claim `the same autonomous capture coalition restores a persistent area-coverage controller` is not established by P0046.

## 2. Is capture-triggered coalition dissolution clearly anteceded?

**Yes at the primitive level.**

- P0043 explicitly states strike completion→temporary coalition dissolution→UAVs become uncommitted.
- P0044 provides true capture plus capture-triggered functional release/reassignment of SWAT resources, although SCREEN did not locate an exact sentence calling this post-capture step `coalition dissolution`.
- P0045 explicitly dissolves coalitions at subtask completion, but its `capture` semantics are generic temporal-task completion.

[AI判断] A novelty claim based only on “completion-triggered coalition release/dissolution” is therefore indefensible.

## 3. Is repeated-arrival resource reuse clearly anteceded?

**Yes.**

P0043 is the cleanest structural example: strike→dissolve→uncommitted reusable UAVs while destroyed targets are replaced by pop-up targets. P0048 cycles the same patrollers through repeated pursuits, while P0051 shows repeated arbitrary arrivals with true terminal capture.

## 4. Does any one paper implement the full same-pool lifecycle?

**No.**

No retained paper simultaneously satisfies all of:

`persistent area coverage/search`
+ `same fungible pool`
+ `temporary capture coalition`
+ `terminal capture`
+ `completion-triggered release`
+ `same-agent restoration of nominal coverage/search`
+ `repeated new arrivals`.

The closest failure modes are complementary:

- P0043 has release+reuse+repeat, but nominal state is uncommitted rather than coverage and task is destructive strike;
- P0044 has dynamic coalition+actual capture+reuse, but SCOUT/SWAT roles are fixed and capture agents return to SWAT tasks, not exploration;
- P0046 has actual apprehension+resume-patrol semantics, but not autonomous repeated temporary-coalition cycling;
- P0048 has the same-pool patrol→pursuit→patrol→repeat loop, but no terminal capture.

## 5. Strongest structural competitor

**P0043 — Liu 2025.**

It most cleanly closes `completion→dissolution→reusable pool→replacement arrivals`. Its decisive distinction from CoCap is `uncommitted ≠ coverage/search`.

## 6. Strongest capture-specific competitor

**P0044 — Chen, Tang & Guo 2024 (KS-COAL).**

It combines actual target immobilization, dynamic capture coalitions, online reassignment, concurrent exploration/defense roles and hardware evidence. Its decisive distinctions are fixed SCOUT/SWAT roles, strong target-information sharing, and no verified repeated target-arrival process.

## 7. Cleanest return-to-patrol predecessor

**P0046 — Bradshaw et al. 2008** for the **capture/apprehension-linked** claim.

P0047 is the cleaner control-theoretic `patrol→event response→patrol` cycle, but it has no capture and therefore cannot by itself kill a post-capture claim.

## 8. Lifecycle gap judgment

**`PARTIALLY SURVIVES`.**

- Not `DEAD`: the integrated same-pool coverage→capture→release→coverage→repeat system was not found.
- Not `STRONGLY SURVIVES`: almost every individual lifecycle edge is already anteceded by one or more papers in complementary settings.

[AI判断] The defensible novelty boundary must therefore be phrased as an **integrated coupling**, ideally further narrowed by CoCap's unresolved local target belief and capturability-aware state-dependent recruitment, rather than as a generic lifecycle novelty.

## 9. Minimal READ set

**P0043 + P0044 + P0046 + P0048**.

- P0043: proves explicit completion→dissolve→reuse→repeat.
- P0044: strongest actual-capture/dynamic-coalition/reuse competitor and hardware anchor.
- P0046: historical capture/apprehension→post-success patrol boundary.
- P0048: closest same-pool persistent patrol↔pursuit recovery under repeated arrivals.

P0045/P0047/P0050/P0051 are sufficient as MAP-level boundary supports after SCREEN; P0049 is an ARCHIVE false positive.
