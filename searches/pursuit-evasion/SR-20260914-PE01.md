### Search question
The organizing question was not “which algorithm is used?” but:

> Under what dynamics, speed-ratio, information-pattern, cardinality, and environment assumptions can pursuers guarantee interception/capture or deny an evader a target/winning region?

Geometric encirclement was kept separate from adversarial PE. Papers were tagged as F1 bridge candidates only when enclosure/encirclement was tied to a capture/winning statement rather than being the terminal objective itself.

## CONFLICTS / UNCERTAINTIES

1. **`capture region`, `winning region`, `reach-avoid set`, `dominance region` are not interchangeable.** A capture/winning region is strategy- and game-definition-dependent; a geometric dominance region is often a time-to-reach comparison in physical space; HJI/viability sets may live in full joint state space.
2. **`barrier` vs `separatrix`.** In Isaacs/reach-avoid work, a barrier commonly denotes a semipermeable boundary between winning regions. “Separatrix” is sometimes used informally for a boundary but should not be asserted equivalent without checking the paper’s definition.
3. **`dominance region` depends on information pattern.** Huang et al. 2025 shows that open-loop reachability of an initial dominance region does not automatically imply a non-anticipative pursuer can keep the evader inside it in the presence of obstacles.
4. **Apollonius geometry is assumption-sensitive.** The familiar circle result is clean for simple motion, fixed speed ratio, obstacle-free full information, and usually point capture. Positive capture radius can make Cartesian ovals more appropriate; obstacles can destroy the naive invariant/winning interpretation.
5. **Reach-avoid PE ≠ classical “avoid capture as long as possible.”** Reach-avoid adds an evader/attacker goal region and often produces a game-of-kind barrier separating target-reaching and interception outcomes.
6. **Geometric enclosure ≠ classical encirclement.** Yan et al. 2024 uses `pursuit enclosure function/enclosure region pursuit` as a winning-certificate construction; it should not be read as identical to multi-agent ring/circumnavigation encirclement.
7. **Visibility-based PE ≠ physical capture PE.** Guibas et al. treat eventual seeing/detection of an arbitrarily fast unknown evader; the “winning” event is fundamentally different.
8. **Open-loop optimality ≠ feedback saddle-point optimality.** Von Moll et al. 2020 explicitly show this distinction in MPSE. Later comparisons must record which solution concept a guarantee actually uses.
9. **Speed-ratio claims require geometry/cardinality/environment qualifiers.** A faster pursuer assumption makes 1v1 simple-motion capture geometry straightforward, but multiple slower pursuers can capture a faster evader under sufficiently favorable initial enclosure conditions (Fang 2022; Zhou 2026), while equal-speed capture can become guaranteed in bounded arenas with enough pursuers (Zhou et al. 2020).
10. **Publication year ambiguity:** some IEEE papers have early-online years preceding issue/print years (e.g., Fang et al. online 2020 / issue 2022; Garcia et al. online 2020 / issue 2021). Canonical index should prefer formal issue metadata while preserving early-online status in notes.
